Steinberg Displacement Limits

Will a component on a circuit board survive vibration and shock? Steinberg's method limits how far the board may flex under the component so its solder joints and leads last 20 million stress cycles (Steinberg, Vibration Analysis for Electronic Equipment). Fill in the inputs on the left; the results and the animation on the right update as you type.
Inputs Your board and component

1 Board and component size

2 Component type

Sets the component constant C. Stiffer attachments get a larger C and a smaller allowable displacement.

3 Where it sits on the board

Sets the position factor r. The board bends most at its center, so r = 1 there.

4 Design life

Stress cycles the part must survive. 20,000,000 is Steinberg's reference; other values use Irvine's fatigue curve N·Z6.4 = const.

5 Vibration and shock load (optional, for margin of safety)

Leave blank to see the limits only. Typical circuit boards: fn 100 to 500 Hz, PSD 0.01 to 0.1 G²/Hz, shock 20 to 100 G.

References: D. S. Steinberg, Vibration Analysis for Electronic Equipment, 3rd ed., Wiley, 2000 · T. Irvine, "Extending Steinberg's Fatigue Analysis of Electronics Equipment Methodology to a Full Relative Displacement vs. Cycles Curve," 2013. When unsure, the conservative choices C = 2.25 and r = 1 give the smallest (safest) allowable displacement.
